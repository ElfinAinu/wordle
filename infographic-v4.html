III

Guess-3 reference

Hit-count table

The rule: Count your hits to find the candidate pool, then play the word that leaves the fewest expected candidates: a candidate if that word is still possible, otherwise a split word that reuses a hit letter or a burn word that tests only fresh letters.

Five hits pins every letter, but it is not always solved: ARISE/RAISE and RESIN/RISEN both return the same board (4 of 2,309 games), so play either — the other is your next guess.

Fig. 03 — Costly mistakes

Avoid

  • Tunnel vision on a single candidate.
  • Reusing letters the board already marked gray.
  • Changing your opener from game to game.

Do

  • Split candidates with a deliberate burn word.
  • Treat every gray as free information.
  • Watch the -E / -ER / -Y endings.
  • Expect a repeat: 32.4% of answers repeat a letter. Only 14.7% double it adjacently; the rest hide the second copy, and 10.3% repeat a vowel apart.

To tell one copy of a letter from two, your guess must carry that letter twice: the number of non-gray tiles among those two copies is the number of copies in the answer. Radar repeats A apart, and SOARE carries a single A, so it can only ever show one hit; guess ABACK and both A tiles come back non-gray, exposing the second copy.

Pick an opener today and never change it.

A fixed opening turns a guessing game into a small, repeatable procedure. The thinking happens once, at the start; the rest is arithmetic.
